## User intent comes first

The user's explicit style, palette, font and mood requests outrank the concept seed, industry conventions and examples below. `visual_vibe` records the requested look, not a suggestion. A professional site can be brutalist, playful, austere or experimental; "professional" does not mean a conventional marketing layout. If a seed detail conflicts with the brief, discard that detail and develop the requested aesthetic. Demonstrate the style through composition, typography, density and imagery, not just its name or a surface texture.

## Design Grounding
The requested style supplies the formal visual language; the business supplies content. Describe how the actual arrangement, letterforms, color relationships and image medium make the style recognizable without its name. Do not require colors to come from an industry object or a fictional physical room. Recognizable style characteristics are not clichés to eliminate. Originality means developing those characteristics for this site, not suppressing them in favor of neutral photography and familiar marketing sections.

## Image Grade — Shared Image Art Direction

Every image on the site is generated independently. The direction MUST include an `image_grade`: one compact, concrete art-direction sentence that gives the imagery a coherent visual language suited to the requested style and subject. Photography is one choice, not a requirement: illustration, painting, collage-like artwork and rendered imagery can also carry the concept. Commit to:

- **Color vs monochrome** — say it explicitly; never leave it ambiguous
- **Medium and treatment** — photographic light and lens character, illustrated line work and shading, painted brushwork, or another intentional rendering approach appropriate to the concept
- **Texture / era cues** — materials, tonal range, mark-making, grain or their deliberate absence, where relevant; film grain is not a universal requirement

Write the grade in the vocabulary of THIS direction's visual tradition, not as a generic film-stock formula. It must fit the concept and palette without forcing different subjects into the same scene. For non-photographic imagery, describe the intended medium rather than imposing photographic grain or camera language.

## Anti-Patterns — What to Avoid

Do NOT produce a direction that feels like generic AI output:
- **Generic palettes**: an unmotivated gradient behind a headline, a mid-tone hue on white with grey supporting text and nothing else committed, arbitrary rainbow accents. This is about the TREATMENT, not the hue: no color is off-limits here. A deep aubergine ground with bone text, ink blue with brass, Klein blue as a flat field — all specific, all welcome. What reads as generic is a color chosen with nothing behind it, and reflexive warm cream is the most common version of that, not the exception to it.
- **Unconsidered font pairings**: choose the display and body roles together. Familiar families are valid when their pairing and use serve this concept; do not choose or reject a face solely because it is familiar.
- **Reflex fonts**: Archivo, Archivo Black, Playfair Display, Cormorant Garamond and Fraunces are not banned, but they are the five this brief will pull you toward hardest — across audited builds those five set more than half of all generated sites, on every kind of topic. Reach for one only when the committed letterform tradition genuinely lands there and you can say what makes it right for THIS site. Google Fonts ships hundreds of families in every tradition; a face chosen because it is familiar is the typographic version of a reflexive warm cream ground.
- **Irrelevant content**: keep image subjects, copy and information hierarchy relevant to this site. A visual tradition can serve many industries; that is not a reason to replace the requested style.

## Calibration — the looks every model lands on

Generated interfaces cluster on a small set of default looks regardless of subject, and a list of them here would only hand you a menu. Where the brief leaves the aesthetic free, landing in a look you have seen a hundred times means the self-check failed. Two tests: if someone could guess your direction from the site's CATEGORY alone, rework it — and if they could guess it from the category plus the obvious avoidance ("not the pale default, so near-black"), rework it too.

A warm, bookish, or hand-made subject does not soften this. Book cloth, thread, jackets, endpapers and shelf ephemera span the whole saturated spectrum; cream paper is the smallest corner of that world. Landing on the subject's most expected material palette is the default wearing the subject's clothes.

## Subject anchor and tension — the two commitments that make this direction this site's

Before you write the palette, answer two questions to yourself and then commit the answers as fields. They are the difference between a direction and a default, and the build records a direction that skips them as delivered plainer than its own concept.

**Subject anchor.** State what connects the imagery or information hierarchy to this particular offering. The swap test applies to relevant content, not to whether a color could suit another business. You need not invent a physical object, room, product material or palette origin. Keep the requested visual language intact while making its content specific.

**Tension.** Describe a useful relationship in the composition: contrasts or deliberate harmony in scale, density, color, type and imagery. It need not mix conflicting traditions. Never dilute the user's style merely to manufacture a surprising pairing.

## Output Format

**The direction must be completely self-contained.** It will be sent to separate model calls in isolation — describe all visual choices explicitly rather than assuming shared context.

Besides the vivid narrative, the direction commits to explicit structured fields. Downstream steps EXECUTE these fields verbatim instead of re-interpreting the prose, so they must agree with the description (same hexes, same font names).

**Commit to an integrated visual direction.** Express the requested style through image choice and composition, palette, typography, spacing, and the supported layout and material treatments. Describe concrete choices in `style_signature`: which subjects and viewpoints belong, how their colors relate to the page, what the letterforms and proportions communicate. Do not treat a style name as an instruction to attach decorative shapes to every section. The image, type and layout choices should carry the direction together.

The image-generation pipeline can depict figurative illustrations, hand-drawn filigree, crests, custom visual marks and other artwork when they serve the concept. Describe these in the intended image subjects and shared image art direction, not as promises of CSS-drawn ornaments. Keep subjects relevant to the site; a stylistic reference should not replace the actual business, product or people with unrelated themed props. Essential headings, labels, navigation and controls remain real HTML for accessibility and reliable wording, even though image models can render lettering. Aesthetic restraint is not universal, and neither is ornamentation.

- `palette`: the six named hexes the theme will ship. `base` = page background, `band` = the large-area tinted-section surface, `contrast` = body text on base (strong contrast required), `primary` = main brand color, `secondary` = supporting small-text color, `accent` = a strong emphasis color. Palette roles are useful defaults, not a ban on large chromatic fields: primary, accent and other readable pairings may form structural color areas when the requested style calls for them. Keep the main action visually identifiable. Choose these colors together with the image art direction, so the imagery and page belong to the same concept. Preserve a clear primary action hierarchy. `band` must stay in the same tint family and on the same light/dark side as `base`, with HSL lightness 6–14 points away (aim for 10). The build verifies this relation and deterministically replaces a missing or drifting band from the delivered base.
- `color_economy`: the palette's hue budget — one of `"monochrome"`, `"single-accent"`, or `"multicolor"`. The chosen seed already committed this: **{{color_economy}}**. Echo it. In `monochrome`, all six semantic roles stay neutral or within one hue family and differ by lightness/chroma; there is no counter-hue accent. In `single-accent`, `base`, `band`, `contrast`, `primary`, and `secondary` form a neutral or tonal family while `accent` is the sole independent hue. In `multicolor`, several hues are intentional, but each has a stable role. These are color budgets, not alternate palette schemas: always return all six roles.
- `ground_key`: whether the page is built up from a pale ground or down from a dark one — `"light"` or `"dark"`. The chosen concept seed already committed this: **{{ground_key}}**. Echo that value and make `palette.base` genuinely light or dark to match. This is checked by WCAG relative luminance after you answer: a contradictory `base` is moved across the light/dark boundary, so do not describe or balance the palette around a ground that will not ship.
- `ground_tint`: which family the page background belongs to — one of `"warm"`, `"cool"`, `"violet"`, `"green"`, `"blush"`, `"neutral"`. The chosen concept seed already committed this: **{{ground_tint}}**. Echo that value and make `palette.base` genuinely belong to it. This is checked deterministically after you answer: a `base` outside the committed family is moved onto it at equal luminance, so a mismatch does not survive — it just means the hex you reasoned about is not the hex that ships. `base` is the largest surface on the page; it is the site's color, not a neutral stage for it. Warm off-white is one of six answers, not the safe one.
- `subject_anchor`: one sentence, per the Subject anchor section above, naming which palette role(s) are taken literally from the subject's own physical world and from what. It MUST name at least one role by its slug (`base`, `band`, `contrast`, `primary`, `secondary`, `accent`) and MUST agree with the hexes you commit in `palette`. The build checks that a role is named; an anchor naming none, or an empty one, is recorded as a delivered defect and the direction ships unanchored.
- `tension`: one sentence, per the Tension section above, naming the one deliberate contrast the direction is built on — both halves, both executable in the vocabulary. The build records an empty tension as a delivered defect; downstream prompts receive the sentence as a fact.
- `style_signature`: describe the concrete image choices, palette relationships, typography and composition that make this interpretation belong to the requested style and subject. Name what a visitor should see, not just the style label. Place shared medium, light and color treatment in `image_grade`, and keep both fields consistent. Downstream authors receive this guidance; it is not a checklist of ornaments or a reason to add visual marks to every band.
- `type`: heading, body, and optional `accent` typography as structured objects. Each `family` MUST be a real Google Fonts family spelled exactly (e.g. "Source Serif 4", "Oswald", "Caveat"), and `weights` MUST list every 100-step weight the direction commits to using. Set `italic` to true only when the design calls for the family's real italic face. `axes` is either `{}` or an `opsz` range shaped exactly as `{ "opsz": { "min": 9, "max": 144 } }`; commit an optical-size range only when the chosen Google family supports it. Keep the visual rationale in `character` so downstream design prompts retain the typographic voice. `type.accent` is OPTIONAL: a script, condensed, or mono face for flavor names, prices, folio, or numerals — never body copy. Leave `accent.family` as `""` when the seed does not need a third face. The build loads every committed family from Google Fonts; never name Druk, Canela, GT Sectra, or other unavailable foundry fonts. Unless the user's requested style or typography calls for a different weight, set grotesque or geometric display fonts for product, portfolio, and technical concepts at weight 500 or 600. Use tight letter spacing. Reserve weights 700 and 800 for poster, pop-culture, and brutalist concepts. Author headings in sentence case. The vocabulary excludes Title Case.
- `type_scale`: the modular type hierarchy — one of `"compact"`, `"classic"`, `"editorial"`, `"dramatic"`, `"brutal"`. Commit it from the concept's typographic voice, not from page length: `compact` keeps display near 2.5rem for archives and catalogs; `classic` is balanced and familiar; `editorial` opens a publication-like hierarchy; `dramatic` creates a forceful 8rem masthead; `brutal` is the rare extreme, reaching 12rem over a 1rem body for concepts built on confrontational scale. The build derives all six font-size presets from this commitment and one body anchor, so do not put literal sizes in the narrative.
- `type_treatment`: the site-wide heading case and tracking language — one of `"sentence"`, `"tight"`, `"caps-tight"`, `"caps-tracked"`, `"lowercase"`. `sentence` preserves authored sentence case at gently tight -0.01em tracking; `tight` preserves sentence case at very tight -0.04em for the product, developer-tool, and technical display voice seen across modern software homepages (pair it with a grotesque or geometric heading face at weight 500 or 600 and a display line-height near 1.0); `caps-tight` uses compact uppercase at -0.03em; `caps-tracked` uses archival/technical uppercase at +0.08em; `lowercase` uses expressive lowercase at +0.01em. Read this decision from the concept's typographic voice instead of defaulting to tight display type. The build writes `textTransform` and `letterSpacing` exactly while preserving the theme model's heading `lineHeight`; never put a competing case or tracking instruction in the narrative.
- `image_kind`: what the site's generated pictures ARE — one of `"photo"`, `"3d-object"`, `"ui-mockup"`, `"line-illustration"`, `"abstract-gradient"`. `photo` is one graded photographic series; `3d-object` is smooth matte clay-like objects and geometric forms in studio light on plain backdrops (playful portfolios, product launches); `ui-mockup` is contemporary product screens: layered panels, charts and tiles drawn as real interface components with soft placeholder bars for text, in a light or dark interface that follows the page ground (SaaS and app landings); `line-illustration` is single-weight line drawings with two or three flat colours (friendly product and fintech pages); `abstract-gradient` is soft grainy gradient fields (studios and dark product pages). Commit it from the brief and the concept: a brief that names mockups, 3D objects, illustrations or gradients has decided it. The build appends the kind's render instruction to every image request and the section authors use its style keyword; `image_grade` still says how the pictures are lit.
- `image_grade`: the one-sentence shared image art direction per the Image Grade section above; use medium-appropriate language for photographs, illustrations or other imagery.
- `image_treatment`: the one render-time treatment applied consistently to delivered content imagery — one of `"natural"`, `"duotone"`, `"tinted-overlay"`, `"high-key-bw"`. Commit it together with `image_grade`: `natural` keeps the generated pixels untouched and is the honest default when the grade already belongs on the palette; `duotone` maps shadows/highlights to the delivered contrast/base pair and must agree with an explicitly two-color or monochrome grade; `tinted-overlay` lays a low-opacity primary tint over Cover and card media and suits color photography that needs gentle palette binding; `high-key-bw` forces bright low-contrast grayscale and is valid only when `image_grade` explicitly commits to monochrome/grayscale/black-and-white imagery. Never describe a natural full-color grade and then choose a destructive monochrome treatment.
- `image_crop`: the site's repeated image-proportion system — one of `"landscape"`, `"portrait"`, `"square"`, `"panoramic"`, `"mixed"`. The build executes this through the documented card/thumbnail/feature-media crop hooks and also composes generated source images for the same target shape. `landscape` is a consistently horizontal photographic system; `portrait` is an editorial vertical system; `square` is a disciplined equal-frame system; `panoramic` is a shallow cinematic system whose feature bands reach 21:9; `mixed` keeps today's role-specific 3:2 / 4:5 / 1:1 variety and is legitimate only when the concept genuinely needs varied proportions. Choose the proportion already implied by the concept's photographic language and keep it compatible with the selected hero blueprint. Full-bleed hero backgrounds remain wide under every value so the viewport never crops a vertical source into a banner.
- `motion`: how the page MOVES — one of `"calm"`, `"energetic"`, `"dramatic"`, `"minimal"`, `"none"`. The theme ships fixed, hand-tuned motion families, not one animation at different speeds: `calm` = soft fades and gentle settling; `energetic` = quick vertical arrivals with a crisp settle and livelier hover; `dramatic` = long vertical masks, a soft-focus hero pull, and cinematic image movement; `minimal` = hover micro-interactions only, no scroll motion; `none` = completely static. Every family moves vertically and settles without overshoot; none of them slides content in from the side. Pick the movement language that serves the concept instead of defaulting to `calm`; each of the five is a legitimate commitment when the concept's world moves that way.
- `motion_note`: a LIST of motion-kit class names the profile should favor, or `[]` when the profile alone says enough. These are class names, not art direction: write a JSON list of class names from the vocabulary below, never a prose description of the movement. Pick from `reveal`, `reveal-up`, `reveal-fade`, `reveal-scale`, `reveal-blur`, `reveal-wipe`, `reveal-wipe-up`, `reveal-aperture`, `reveal-zoom`, `stagger-children`, `ken-burns`, `gradient-shift`, `ambient-drift` (ambient — at most one for the whole page), `hover-lift`, `hover-reveal` (hover — at most one). Treat this list as the site's motion palette: name the two or three entrance classes whose character belongs to the concept's visual world, the way the palette commits hexes. Each class has its own character (a mask, a focus pull, a zoom, a fade, a rise, a stagger); read that character off the concept's visual world and pick the classes that carry it. Two directions with different worlds should end up with visibly different lists. Downstream sections choose from this palette first, so an empty list forfeits the site's motion identity to per-section defaults. Never name `hero-entrance`; the hero owns it. Per-block pairs that fight over the same transform (`ambient-drift`+`hover-lift`, `ken-burns`+`hover-reveal`) are motion-sanity's job, not this list. A `minimal` profile may name only the two hover classes, and `none` may name nothing. Anything outside this vocabulary is dropped and recorded.
- `surface`: an optional section texture. Choose `"none"`, `"paper"`, `"concrete"`, `"film"`, `"fabric"`, `"noise"`, or `"dot-grid"`. Start with `none`. Choose a texture only when a specific material or visual process in this concept needs it. A restaurant, portfolio, bakery, or festival does not need a texture because of its category. Warm colors, dark colors, an editorial typeface, and a request for visual interest are insufficient reasons. Prefer a plain background when photographs or typography already express the concept. `noise` supplies fine irregular grain. `dot-grid` supplies a sparse grid. The build supplies the texture below the content of at most one suitable section per page. The hero, header, footer, tables, forms, and most sections remain plain. A page can use no texture. Describe a texture only when this field selects it.
- `surface_reason`: one concrete sentence that links the texture to the concept and explains which content benefits from it. State why a plain section would express that concept less clearly. Use an empty string for `none`. The build replaces a texture with `none` when this reason is absent.
- `device`: an optional one-band CSS ornament — one of `"none"`, `"hairline-rule"`, `"stamp"`. The page plan may place it on at most one non-hero band. Leave `"none"` unless the concept needs that one mark. There is no numeral device: sequence numbers ("01", "02") on bands or items are banned unless the SITE BRIEF explicitly asks for visible numbering. Twine, tape, and illustrated motifs are not devices.
- `canvas`: `"full-bleed"` or `"framed"` — how the page meets the viewport edge. `full-bleed` (the default) lets heroes, image bands and color bands run edge-to-edge. Commit to `framed` ONLY when the concept genuinely calls for a contained, gallery-mat presentation — the page then keeps a visible mat of page background around every band below the fold, and the header can never float over the hero. The page-opening hero is always exempt from the mat: it runs edge-to-edge on every canvas (a hero stopped short of the viewport edge reads as a rendering bug, not a mat), and the frame begins with the second band. Don't pick `framed` as a hedge; an accidental frame reads as a rendering bug, not a design choice.
- `measure`: the paired reading/stage width — one of `"narrow"`, `"standard"`, `"wide"`, `"full"`. `narrow` = 640/1000px for art-books, poetry, and single-column editorial; `standard` = 860/1320px for balanced general use; `wide` = 960/1560px for dense product and catalog pages; `full` = 1040/1760px for galleries and screen-filling concepts. Choose `measure` and `canvas` together: on a `framed` canvas the committed stage width is the visible mat edge below the hero, so a contained art-book direction usually needs `narrow` or `standard`, while a full-screen gallery may pair `full` with `full-bleed`. The build writes the pair exactly; never put competing widths in the narrative.
- `card_style`: how the site's cards are constructed — one of `"flush"`, `"framed"`, `"overlap"`, `"borderless"`. `flush` (the default) bleeds card media to the card's edges with padding only around the text — the contemporary look; pick it unless the concept argues otherwise. `framed` insets the media behind padding on all sides — commit to it ONLY when the concept genuinely calls for that framing; as an accidental default it reads dated. `overlap` rides the text panel up over the media's bottom edge. `borderless` drops the card box entirely — media above a plain text stack, whitespace as the only separator. Read the construction off the concept's own world, not off its subject category.
- `item_pattern`: the preferred repeated-content idiom — one of `"card"`, `"rule-row"`, `"spec-table"`, `"tag-cluster"`. Cards group related content, rule rows support scanning, specification tables pair labels with facts, and tag clusters collect short labels. Pick a useful starting point from the site's content. The planner may choose another supported idiom per section when its information calls for it; shared typography, palette and spacing supply coherence.
- `depth`: how cards and contained media sit on the page — one of `"flat"`, `"ring"`, `"soft"`, `"hard-offset"`, `"inset"`, `"glow"`, `"glass"`. This is a literal build-owned treatment: `flat` removes elevation; `ring` draws one 1px hairline ring mixed from `contrast` with no lift; `soft` adds a restrained diffuse lift; `hard-offset` adds a crisp offset plate; `inset` presses surfaces inward; `glow` adds a primary-colored halo; `glass` turns band-coloured cards into frosted panels (a translucent tint of `band` over a blurred page, one light hairline, a deep soft drop) and is ONLY for a dark ground: commit it when the brief names glass cards or the concept is a dark product or studio page with panels floating over a photo or gradient; on a light ground the build degrades it to `ring`. The build applies the committed value once to card shells, contained images, contained covers, and media-text surfaces; full-bleed media stays unelevated. Depth is independent of `card_style`: a borderless card may still give its contained image depth, and a framed card may still sit flat. Read the treatment off the concept's physical and light language, not off its subject category. `flat` is a positive design commitment when the concept explicitly wants no elevation, not an absence, hedge, or fallback. Do not promise additional bespoke shadow recipes in the narrative.
- `cta_style`: the site-wide call-to-action construction — one of `"solid"`, `"outline"`, `"underline"`, `"ghost-arrow"`, `"block"`. `solid` is a filled accent button; `outline` is a transparent 2px outline; `underline` is an unboxed text action with a strong underline; `ghost-arrow` is unboxed text followed by an arrow glyph; `block` is a square, bordered contrast slab that fills only a narrow container (a card or a column at most one third of the content width) and otherwise keeps its intrinsic width. Choose `block` only when the USER PROMPT itself asks for a brutalist, poster, or heavy typographic register; a concept seed that names posters is not enough. A brief that asks for minimal, restrained, quiet, refined, or generous whitespace takes `underline`, `outline`, or `solid`, never `block`. Read the choice from the concept instead of defaulting every subject to a filled uppercase pill. The build owns button fill, border, padding, interaction states, container-width behavior, and the ghost arrow; do not promise a competing construction in the narrative. Button typography and the separate `shape` radius remain their own choices.
- `section_label`: whether non-hero sections name themselves — one of `"none"`, `"section-badge"`, `"side-label"`. Eyebrows and kickers are banned; these are the two committed forms in which a small label returns, never in the hero or a page opening, never twice in a section. `section-badge` is a pill with a dot and one or two words ("Use cases", "Process") directly above each section heading; the build paints the pill from the committed shape and accent. Commit it for product landings, SaaS pages and portfolios in the current web idiom whose sections are scanned by topic. `side-label` puts the same one or two words in the leading column of a split beside the section's heading stack (the agency and studio idiom: a quiet uppercase caption that stays in view while the content scrolls); the build paints it. Commit it for editorial agency, studio and portfolio concepts whose sections read as a ledger of chapters. Leave `none` for heritage and quiet concepts, where the heading is the first line.
- `heading_emphasis`: how ONE clause inside a heading is set apart from the rest — one of `"none"`, `"two-tone"`, `"italic-word"`, `"highlight"`. Executed literally by the build on a clause the section author marks. Read it off the letterform tradition and the brief, not off habit — every cohort of five briefs was answering `two-tone` five times, and that is the reflex this field exists to break: `italic-word` swaps one to three words into italic, in the accent face when `type.accent` commits one; it is the natural answer for a `display-serif`, `didone`, `humanist` or `script` letterform tradition, for a playful or editorial concept, and for any brief that names an italic or serif word. `highlight` draws a translucent accent highlighter band behind one to three words; it is the natural answer for a friendly product, fintech, education or consumer concept on a light ground with a real accent hue. `two-tone` mutes the quieter clause of a heading to 70% ink beside the strong clause; it belongs to a dark product landing, an agency or a portfolio set in a `grotesque`, `geometric` or `condensed` face, and it is NOT the default for everything else. `none` is a real commitment for concepts whose headings are one tone (quiet, heritage, archival). A direction that promises two-tone or italic headings in its narrative must commit the matching value here, or the page ships one-tone headings.
- `shape`: the corner language for contained media (`core/image`, `core/cover`, the media half of `core/media-text`) and buttons — `"sharp"`, `"soft"`, or `"round"`. One commitment for the whole site, executed literally by the build: `sharp` keeps contained media and buttons square; `soft` gives contained media a subtle radius and buttons a modest one; `round` gives contained media a decisive radius and buttons a pill shape. Full-bleed media always meets its edges square, whatever the shape. Generic card wrappers are outside this deterministic commitment, so do not promise their geometry from this field. This is a real decision with no safe value: read it off the concept's own visual world. Read the corner language off the geometry the concept's own world is made of. Both mismatches read as bugs — rounding that nothing in the direction motivates reads as template styling, and reflexive squareness on a concept whose world is genuinely rounded reads as an unfinished direction. Never pick any of the three as a hedge; the corner language must be one the description itself already speaks.
- `rhythm`: the page's overall sequence — one of `"stacked"`, `"alternating"`, `"offset"`, `"interrupted"`, `"banded"`, `"gallery"`. A steady reading column, alternating compositions, asymmetric offsets, a deliberate interruption, color-led fields or image-led sequencing are all valid. Choose the rhythm the concept needs. Repetition is intentional when it carries the content; no count of archetypes or colored bands defines quality.
- `density`: how tightly the page packs vertically — one of `"expansive"`, `"airy"`, `"measured"`, `"dense"`, `"packed"`. Sections carry their own compact/standard/spacious assignment, and the build also scales the physical spacing scale from this commitment: the `lg`/`xl`/`xxl` section-padding ramp, the `xs`/`sm`/`md` component spacing, and the page's inline gutter all move together (the component swing is deliberately smaller). `airy` suits gallery and luxury concepts, `dense` suits catalogs, archives and editorial concepts with a lot to say. The two extremes are deliberate commitments, not stronger synonyms: `expansive` is monumental emptiness for a concept whose world is genuinely carried by silence — a single sculptural product, a photographer's darkened stage, monumental editorial — where whole screens may hold one object; `packed` is maximal compression for a concept that is honestly an index — a wholesale catalog, a schedule, a directory, a data-dense archive — where the reader came to scan, not to pause. Pick an extreme only when the concept's own description already demands it; a middle value chosen as a hedge and an extreme chosen for drama are the same mistake.
- `text_placement`: the site-level horizontal intent for readable copy below page-opening heroes — one of `"left-column"`, `"centered"`, `"split"`, `"asymmetric-thirds"`. This moves the copy column, never its readable measure. `left-column` starts most copy stacks on a wide band's leading edge; `centered` centers the column while keeping multi-line paragraphs start-aligned; `split` makes copy one side of an intentional two-zone composition and may alternate sides down the page; `asymmetric-thirds` offsets copy into the second or third zone of wide bands. Pick the axis the concept actually needs rather than leaving every band on the reflexive leading edge. `hero_blueprint.text_anchor` remains the separate authority for the front-page hero.
- `header_chrome`: whether the header stays on screen after the reader scrolls — one of `"persistent"` or `"transient"`. `persistent` keeps the header available at the top of the viewport for the whole page; `transient` lets the header leave with the opening composition and meet the reader again at the top of the page. This is a real decision with no safe value, and it is not a platform default: a bar that never leaves takes viewport height from every band below it and lays one horizontal strip across the composition this direction spends its budget on. Commit to `persistent` only when this site passes every one of these tests: the reader has several destinations to move between; the reader needs them part-way down a page, not only at its end; the page is long enough that the reader loses the top of it; and the header this direction describes is compact enough that a fixed strip costs little height. Commit to `transient` when any one test fails. Read the answer off the reading task this site gives its reader and off the header you just described, never off the site's subject category. The build applies its own vetoes on top of the commitment: a tall header archetype and a site with too little depth never receive persistent chrome, whatever this field says.
